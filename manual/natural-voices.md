# Natural offline voices

**Pro.**

Anyone can hear the voices first: in Settings → Reading Vault, press ▶ next to a voice to play a short sample. Samples play straight away, with no big download (each is a small clip, downloaded the first time you play it).

Reading Vault Pro can read your books with 28 natural-sounding English voices, American and British, that sound much closer to a person reading than your computer's own voices. They run on your own computer, on Mac and Windows, with no internet needed once they're downloaded.

## Setting them up

1. Open Settings → Reading Vault and find **Natural voices (Kokoro)**.
2. Press **Download (325 MB)**. This happens once. The download is kept on your computer, outside your vault, so it doesn't sync to your phone or get backed up with your notes.
3. Tick the voices you want in **Voices to choose from**.
4. Turn them on: switch on **Natural voices** on the listening bar, or pick a natural voice as the **Default voice** in Settings.

Natural voices start switched off when you first get Pro, so Play reads with your computer's voice until you turn them on. After that, press Play in the Reader as usual. The first sentence can take a few seconds while the voice gets ready; the listening bar says "Getting the voice ready…" meanwhile.

If you turn Natural voices on before downloading, Reading Vault asks first: **Download now** starts the one-time download and reads as soon as it's ready, and **Use a Mac voice for now** reads with your computer's own voice until you download. Nothing downloads without you saying so.

## Good to know

- The natural voices read English. Other languages use your computer's own voices. See [Listen](listen.md).
- Without Pro, Listen uses your computer's own voices.
- **Download again** in Settings replaces a missing or damaged download.
- Your computer's own voices stay available with Pro: they're listed under the natural voices, in Settings and in the Reader's voice list.
- The natural voice uses your computer's processor and some memory while it reads. After five minutes without reading it lets go of that memory; the next Play gets it ready again in a few seconds. With two Obsidian windows open, only the window you're listening in keeps it.
